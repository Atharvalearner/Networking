First, the sender compares the destination IP with its own subnet mask. If the destination is on a different network, it does not ARP for the destination host. Instead, it ARPs for the default gateway's MAC address. After receiving the router's MAC, it creates an Ethernet frame with the router as the destination MAC while keeping the original destination IP unchanged. Each router removes the incoming Layer 2 frame, examines the destination IP, consults its routing table, and creates a new Layer 2 frame for the next hop. When the packet reaches the destination network, the final router uses ARP to discover the destination host's MAC address and sends the frame through the switch to the destination device
